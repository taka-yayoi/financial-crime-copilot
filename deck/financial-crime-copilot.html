Financial Crime Copilot2
What changes

Keep every rule and every alert — change which alerts analysts work first, and how fast a SAR gets written

Prioritized queue with reasons

A model scores each alert by how likely it is to be genuinely suspicious. Analysts work top-down; each case shows the three factors that ranked it high.

SAR draft in seconds, filed by a human

For every high-priority case a regulator-style SAR draft is ready. The analyst reviews, edits and files it — nothing is filed automatically.

One cockpit for the analyst

Queue, case detail, SAR editor and disposition buttons in a single app; every decision is recorded with who made it and when.

Answers in plain English for managers

"How many high-priority alerts are still open?" — answered from the same governed data the analysts work on, with the query shown.

Why it is safe to adopt

Designed for the regulator's questions, not around them

Question you will be askedHow the design answers it
Could the model hide a true positive?No alert is removed or auto-closed. The rules still raise every alert; the model only sets the order.
Who is accountable for a SAR?The analyst. AI drafts; a person reviews, edits and files.
Can you explain a ranking to an examiner?Every score ships with its top contributing factors, shown on the case screen.
Who can see customer PII?National IDs and e-mails are masked centrally for anyone outside the authorized analyst group.
Is there an audit trail?Every disposition is stored with analyst and timestamp, and lands in the governed analytics layer.
